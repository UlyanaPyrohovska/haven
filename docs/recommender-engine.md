## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Haven shows each user a small **daily drop** of 4 people (`CADENCE.batchSize`,
`apps/api/src/lib/matching/scoring.ts:43`), refreshed at most every 24 hours. For a viewer *u* the engine
builds a pool of up to 500 eligible people, scores each candidate *v* in **both directions**
(how good *v* is for *u*, and how good *u* is for *v*), combines the two with a **harmonic mean**
so that a pair only ranks high when it works for both sides, gives a small boost to people *u* has
physically **crossed paths** with, then **re-ranks for exposure fairness** so popular profiles do
not take every slot. Up to two people who already liked *u* are guaranteed a slot. The final 4 are
shuffled before display so the order doesn't reveal who liked you.

## 3. When a batch is generated (cadence)

A new batch is generated by `ensureBatch` (`apps/api/src/modules/recommendations/service.ts:92`)
only when **all three** hold:

1. the viewer has **no pending** (not yet acted-on) recommendations,
2. the profile is **complete** (`isProfileComplete`),
3. the **cooldown** has elapsed since the latest `shown_at` (`isCooldownElapsed`,
   `apps/api/src/lib/matching/recommendation-cadence.ts:48`). Default 24 h, overridable by the
   `REC_COOLDOWN_HOURS` env var (`recommendation-cadence.ts:5`) or by the saved matching config.

Triggers:

- **Lazily** on `GET /recommendations` (`service.ts:112`).
- **On onboarding completion**, skipping the cooldown (`maybeGenerateOnProfileComplete`, `service.ts:322`).
- **By the simulation cron** for every real user (`apps/api/src/modules/simulation/engine.ts:110`).
- **Regenerate** (`service.ts:376`): allowed only while the viewer hasn't accepted or declined anyone
  in the current batch. It reuses the batch's `shown_at`, so the cooldown doesn't move and no new
  exposure rows are logged (`repository.ts:419`).
- **Filter changes** don't refill; they retire pending rows that no longer pass the mutual filters
  (`dismissPendingFilterMisfits`, `repository.ts:809`).

## 4. The pipeline, step by step

`generateRecommendations` (`apps/api/src/modules/recommendations/generator.ts:122`):

```
load matching config  ──►  load viewer scoring profile
        │
        ▼
candidate pool (≤ 500): hard filters in SQL, pending likers first,
   200 nearest bios (if viewer has an embedding) + random fill       generator.ts:145
        │
        ▼  (in parallel)                                              generator.ts:153
crossings · pending liker ids · exposure counts (7-day window) · learned preferences
        │
        ▼
score every candidate: reciprocal = harmonic mean of the two one-way scores,
   then cross-paths boost                                             generator.ts:159 (rankCandidates, :74)
        │
        ▼
reserve up to 2 pending likers → exposure-fair rerank → take k = 4    generator.ts:168
        │
        ▼
seeded shuffle for display                                            generator.ts:171
        │
        ▼
replace pending rows in `recommendations` + log `recommendation_exposures`   generator.ts:173
```

The stored `score` is the relevance score **after** the cross-paths boost but **before** the
exposure penalty (`fairness.ts:32`). The stored `rank` is the **display position after the
shuffle**, not the score order. Keep that in mind when you evaluate from the table (section 18).

## 5. Candidate pool and hard filters

`listCandidatePool` (`apps/api/src/modules/recommendations/repository.ts:350`) with the conditions
from `poolConditions` (`repository.ts:303`):

| Filter | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Not yourself | `repository.ts:305` | |
| Discoverable | `repository.ts:306` | `discoverable = true` |
| Same pool | `repository.ts:307` | same `looking_for` (dating and friendship never mix) |
| No prior interaction | `noInteractionWith`, `repository.ts:236` | drops anyone you already decided on, and anyone who passed on you; a pending like **on** you stays so you can answer it |
| Mutual gender and age | `partnerFilterConditions`, `repository.ts:269` | both directions: the candidate fits your `interested_in` and age range, and you fit theirs. Unknown data passes (same rule in TS: `apps/api/src/lib/matching/partner-filters.ts:22` and `:41`) |
| Synthetic only | `repository.ts:312` | only for simulation agents |
| Max distance | `repository.ts:316` | bounding box, **but no caller passes `maxKm`**, so distance is never a hard filter today; it only acts through the geo score |

**Pool composition** (`repository.ts:367-385`):

- Pending likers are always sorted first (`likesViewer`, `repository.ts:255`), so neither the
  limit nor the bio shortlist can hide them.
- If the viewer has a bio embedding: up to `ANN_SHORTLIST_LIMIT = 200` candidates
  (`generator.ts:22`) ordered by pgvector cosine distance `<=>`. The comment at `repository.ts:370`
  explains that the `likedFirst` sort key makes this an **exact** sort, not an HNSW index scan.
- The remaining slots up to `POOL_LIMIT = 500` (`generator.ts:16`) are filled **at random**, so
  profiles without an embedding, or far away in bio space, still get scored and nobody is stuck
  outside the limit forever.

With ~2,000 profiles split over two pools and further cut by gender filters, the pool is often
the whole eligible population, so this is effectively a full scan.

**Profile data for scoring** (`scoringProfileSelect`, `repository.ts:68`; mapping at `:176`):
17 interest columns, custom interests, the 6 `pref_*` values (a 100-point split over attractive,
sincere, intelligent, fun, ambitious, shared interests), the 6 `self_*` ratings (1 to 10),
coordinates, bio embedding, today's mood and today's daily-question answer. **Synthetic profiles**
get a deterministic mood (`syntheticMoodForProfile`, `apps/api/src/lib/daily/candidate-mood.ts:6`) and a
deterministic answer from a fixed list (`apps/api/src/lib/daily/candidate-daily-answer.ts:6`), seeded by
profile id and date.

## 6. The six score terms

Each term is a number in [0, 1], or `null` when the data is missing. Default weights
(`DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`, `apps/api/src/lib/matching/scoring.ts:33`):

| Term | Weight | Function | Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bio similarity | 0.30 | `bioSimilarity` | `scoring.ts:208` |
| Interest similarity | 0.25 | `interestSimilarity` | `scoring.ts:147` |
| Geo proximity | 0.15 | `geoProximity` | `scoring.ts:194` |
| Preference match (directed) | 0.15 | `directedPreferenceMatch` | `scoring.ts:184` |
| Mood compatibility | 0.05 | `moodCompatibility` | `scoring.ts:229` |
| Daily-answer similarity | 0.10 | `dailyAnswerSimilarity` | `scoring.ts:292` |

Four terms are **symmetric** (bio, interest, geo, mood) and two are not: preference is directed
(what *u* wants vs who *v* is), and the daily answer is symmetric in value but only counted when
both answered. Because preference is directed, the two one-way scores differ, which is what makes
reciprocity meaningful.

### 6.1 Interest similarity (`scoring.ts:147`)

- Vectors: the 17 fixed interests (nulls become the neutral 5, `scoring.ts:62`), plus **one extra
  dimension per custom interest** either side holds, valued 10 if held and 1 if not
  (`scoring.ts:64-65`, mirroring what onboarding writes for picked and unpicked interests).
- Similarity: **Pearson correlation mapped to [0, 1]**: `(r + 1) / 2` (`centeredSimilarity`,
  `scoring.ts:117`). Centering compares *which* interests stand out for each person, not their
  overall level, and makes an empty (all-neutral) profile score exactly 0.5 instead of looking like
  a good overlap.

### 6.2 Preference match, directed (`scoring.ts:170`, `:184`)

- `preferenceMatch(u.pref, v.self)`: Pearson-mapped similarity between *u*'s 100-point preference
  split and *v*'s self-ratings. It asks: "are the traits *v* rates highest the ones *u* weights most?"
  Centering the self-ratings makes it immune to someone rating themselves 10 on everything.
  A flat split or flat self-ratings give 0.5.
- `directedPreferenceMatch` blends in the **learned** taste (section 10):
  `(1 − s) · stated + s · preferenceMatch(learnedPref, v.self)`, where `s` is the learned share.
  Blending happens in score space, so a weak learned signal stays weak.

### 6.3 Geo proximity (`scoring.ts:194`)

`exp(−distance_km / 25)` (`GEO_DECAY_TAU_KM = 25`, `scoring.ts:58`), haversine distance.
0 km gives 1.0, 25 km gives 0.37, 50 km gives 0.14. **Unknown location gives 0.5**, not null.

### 6.4 Bio similarity (`scoring.ts:208`)

Cosine similarity of the two 384-dimension bio embeddings (section 11), clamped to [0, 1].
`null` if either side has no embedding.

### 6.5 Mood compatibility (`scoring.ts:220-235`)

A hand-set symmetric 6×6 table over happy, calm, excited, tired, low, stressed. 0.5 is neutral;
calm pairs well with anything (0.6 to 0.7); two low people score 0.2. `null` if either side hasn't
checked in today. In the score it can **only lift** (section 7), so a low mood is a disclosure,
never a penalty.

### 6.6 Daily-answer similarity (`scoring.ts:282-309`)

Both people's answers to today's question are tokenized (lower-case words longer than 2
characters, minus a stopword list at `scoring.ts:238`). The score is `0.5 + 0.5 · Jaccard`, so no
shared words is neutral (0.5) and identical word sets give 1.0. `null` if either side didn't answer.
It is **lexical, not semantic**: the comment at `scoring.ts:290` marks it as a shortcut to replace
with embedding cosine. Like mood, it can only lift.

## 7. Calibration and the one-way score

### 7.1 Why calibrate

The raw terms have very different spreads: on the dev DB the preference term varies with sd 0.22
but the daily answer only 0.07. A raw weighted mean would let high-variance terms dominate no matter
what the weights say. Calibration turns every term into a **z-score**, so a weight means the same
influence for every term.

`COMPONENT_CALIBRATION` (`scoring.ts:318`), measured 2026-10-29 on ~10k random same-pool pairs,
mostly synthetic:

| Term | mean | sd |
|---|---|---|
| interest | 0.605 | 0.119 |
| geo | 0.594 | 0.152 |
| pref | 0.495 | 0.220 |
| bio | 0.462 | 0.107 |
| mood | 0.500 | 0.177 |
| dailyAnswer | 0.512 | 0.070 |

Regenerate it with `pnpm --filter api db:component-stats` (`apps/api/scripts/component-stats.ts`),
which prints the table and a block to paste back into `scoring.ts`.

### 7.2 The one-way score `unilateral(u, v)` (`scoring.ts:340`)

For each term *i* with value *xᵢ*:

```
zᵢ = clip((xᵢ − meanᵢ) / sdᵢ, −3, +3)       missing term  → zᵢ = 0  (exactly neutral)
z_mood   = max(0, z_mood)                   context terms only lift
z_answer = max(0, z_answer)

S = Σ wᵢ · zᵢ / sqrt(Σ wᵢ²)                 standardized: unit spread if terms are independent
p(u→v) = sigmoid(S)                         in (0, 1); 0.5 = an average pair
```

Points worth knowing:

- **Clip at 3σ** (`Z_CLIP`, `scoring.ts:328`): two identical daily answers sit ~7σ out and would
  otherwise swamp everything.
- **Missing equals neutral.** No embedding, no mood check-in or no answer simply contributes 0;
  it never punishes.
- **Weights are scale-free.** Multiplying all weights by a constant changes nothing (the norm divides
  it out). Only ratios matter. The backoffice still requires them to sum to 1 for readability
  (`weightsSumToOne`, `apps/api/src/lib/matching/matching-config.ts:86`).
- **Effective influence.** After calibration each profile term's influence is proportional to its
  weight. Mood and daily answer are one-sided (`max(0, z)`), so their effective spread is roughly
  half of a two-sided term's. Note that the "effectiveShare" column printed by `component-stats.ts`
  is `w · sd` of the **raw** terms, which describes the **uncalibrated** formula.

### 7.3 The uncalibrated formula (ablation)

Passing `calibration = null` uses `uncalibratedUnilateral` (`scoring.ts:378`): a weighted mean of
the four profile terms (a missing bio drops out and the rest renormalize) plus mood and answer as
offsets from 0.5, clamped to [0, 1]. This is the thesis ablation for "is calibration worth it".
The production generator always uses the calibrated default; you choose this only in an offline
script.

## 8. Reciprocity: the harmonic mean (`scoring.ts:413`)

```
p = unilateral(u, v)     how good v is for u
q = unilateral(v, u)     how good u is for v
reciprocal = 2·p·q / (p + q)
```

The harmonic mean is dominated by the **smaller** side. Two examples:

| p | q | arithmetic mean | harmonic mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.80 |
| 0.95 | 0.30 | 0.625 | 0.456 |

So a candidate who is perfect for *u* but would almost certainly pass on *u* ranks low. This is the
core idea of reciprocal recommenders (RECON, Pizzato et al. 2010): in dating, a recommendation only
has value if it can become a **match**.

Both sides' learned preferences are attached before scoring (`generator.ts:157`), since *q* needs
*v*'s taste.

`scoreBreakdown` (`scoring.ts:443`) returns every term separately (with `prefUToV` and `prefVToU`)
for explanations. It feeds the "why you matched" reasons (`describeMatch`, `service.ts:210`;
thresholds at `apps/api/src/lib/matching/match-reasons.ts:14`) and the staff breakdown view.

## 9. Re-ranking

### 9.1 Crossed-paths boost (`scoring.ts:474-486`)

Applied **once to the reciprocal score**, never inside `unilateral`, so it is symmetric:

```
norm(c)   = 1 − exp(−c / K)                       K = 3        (c = crossing count)
boosted   = sigmoid( logit(reciprocal) + λ · norm(c) )         λ = 0.5   (CROSS_PATHS, scoring.ts:52)
```

Working in logit space means the boost is "at most λ standard units" wherever the score sits, so it
helps an average candidate and a top candidate equally. 1 crossing gives 0.28·λ, 3 crossings give
0.63·λ, and it saturates towards λ. λ is kept ≤ 0.5 so it doesn't double-count the geo term.
Crossings where either side has turned off sharing are excluded at read time
(`listCrossings`, `repository.ts:453`), so they count as 0.

### 9.2 Pending likers (`generator.ts:25`, `:95`)

People who liked the viewer and are still waiting:

- are always in the pool (section 5),
- are sorted first in `rankCandidates` (`generator.ts:90`),
- and the **2 best-scoring** of them (`LIKER_SLOTS = 2`) are **reserved**: they skip the fairness
  penalty and the cap and lead the batch. Any other likers compete on fair score like everyone else,
  so a batch is never all likers.

### 9.3 Exposure fairness (`apps/api/src/lib/matching/fairness.ts`)

Goal: spread impressions so a few attractive profiles don't appear in everyone's drop
(individual, provider-side fairness).

- **Exposure count** *e*: impressions of the candidate across **all viewers** in the last 7 days
  (`listExposureCounts`, `repository.ts:433`; window `FAIRNESS.windowDays`, `fairness.ts:10`).
  An impression is a row in `recommendation_exposures`, one per candidate per new batch
  (`repository.ts:425`).
- **Penalty** (`exposurePenalty`, `fairness.ts:23`):

  ```
  fair = sigmoid( logit(score) − μ · e / (e + e₀) )      μ = 1,  e₀ = 5 (or = cap if a cap is set)
  ```

  Saturating: 5 impressions cost μ/2, it can never cost more than μ standard units.
- **Optional hard cap** (`fairness.ts:50`): candidates with `e ≥ cap` only fill slots the uncapped
  ones leave empty. Default `cap = null` (no cap).
- **Sort order** (`fairness.ts:50`): reserved likers first, then uncapped before capped, then by
  `fair`. Take k. The stored score stays the **unpenalized** relevance.

`μ = 0` and `cap = null` reproduce the unfair ranking exactly (thesis ablation).

### 9.4 Display shuffle (`generator.ts:110`, `:171`)

Fisher–Yates seeded by `viewerId:shownAt`, so the order is stable across reloads but reserved
likers aren't always on top (which would reveal who liked you).

### 9.5 What gets written

`replaceRecommendations` (`repository.ts:389`) deletes the viewer's pending rows, upserts the new
ones (`score`, display `rank`), and, for a new batch only, appends exposure rows.

### 9.6 Note on decisions and matches

`recordDecision` (`service.ts:278`) writes one side of the `interactions` row (`dec_a` or `dec_b`,
1 = accept, 0 = pass). A **match** is both sides = 1. The same function is used by the mobile
endpoint (`postDecision`, `service.ts:245`) and by the simulation engine, so agent and human
decisions are stored identically.

## 10. Learned preferences (`apps/api/src/lib/matching/preference-learning.ts`)

The stated 100-point split is what people *say* they want. This module learns what they *actually*
like from their decisions.

1. **Data**: every decision the user made (both orientations of `interactions`), paired with the
   other person's 6 self-ratings (`listPreferenceDecisions`, `repository.ts:551`).
2. Each person's self-ratings are **z-scored within the profile** (`centered`,
   `preference-learning.ts:24`), so "everyone rates themselves high on sincerity" cancels out.
3. **Contrast** `c = mean(z of liked) − mean(z of everyone decided on)` (`likedContrast`,
   `preference-learning.ts:45`). Subtracting everything decided on also cancels the recommender's own
   selection bias (it only ever shows certain kinds of people). No likes or no contrast means nothing
   is learned.
4. **Direction** = `c` z-scored (`learnedPreference`, `preference-learning.ts:62`).
5. **Share** of the preference term (`learnPreference`, `preference-learning.ts:76`):

   ```
   s = λ · n / (n + n₀) · ‖c‖ / (‖c‖ + c₀)        λ = 0.5, n₀ = 10, c₀ = 0.5
   ```

   With 10 decisions and a strong contrast the share is about λ/2 · (≈0.8). Few decisions or a weak
   contrast barely move the score; the share never exceeds λ.
6. The direction and share are attached to the profile (`withLearnedPreferences`, `generator.ts:46`)
   and used by `directedPreferenceMatch` (section 6.2), for the viewer **and** every candidate.

`λ = 0` turns learning off and reproduces the stated-only ranking.

## 11. Bio embeddings (Cloudflare Workers AI)

`apps/api/src/lib/matching/bio-embedding.ts`

- **Model**: `@cf/baai/bge-small-en-v1.5` (`bio-embedding.ts:3`), 384 dimensions
  (`bio-embedding.ts:5`), at most 100 texts per call (`bio-embedding.ts:7`). Bound as `AI` in
  `apps/api/wrangler.jsonc`.
- **Storage**: table `user_embeddings (user_profile_id, bio vector(384))` with an HNSW cosine index
  (`apps/api/src/db/schema.ts:249`, index `:260`). One model only: never mix vectors from two models
  in the same table, since their cosines are not comparable.
- **On profile save** (`apps/api/src/modules/profile/service.ts:82-87`): if the bio changed,
  `syncBioEmbedding` (`bio-embedding.ts:36`) runs in `waitUntil`, off the response path. A blank
  bio deletes the vector. It **never throws**: if Workers AI fails, the bio term is simply `null`
  (neutral) for that user. `embedTexts` (`bio-embedding.ts:19`) validates count and dimension.
- **Backfill**: `POST /staff/embeddings/backfill` (`apps/api/src/modules/staff/service.ts:186`)
  re-embeds every bio in batches of 100 and removes vectors whose bio was cleared. Run it after every
  `db:seed`. Per `docs/FEATURES.md`, it had not yet been run on dev or prod at the time of writing, so
  older rows may still hold vectors from the previous local MiniLM model; check before evaluating
  (section 18).
- **Use in ranking**: (a) the bio term (section 6.4), (b) the 200-candidate nearest-bio shortlist
  (section 5).

## 12. Crossed-paths detection

Constants in `apps/api/src/lib/location/crossings.ts`: radius **1 km** (`:4`), window **30 min** (`:6`),
cooldown **6 h** (`:8`).

1. When a device sends a fresh location (profile update), `recordCrossingsOnLocationUpdate`
   (`service.ts:345`) runs, if the user shares cross paths, doesn't hide their location and has
   coordinates.
2. `recordCrossings` (`repository.ts:506`) finds same-pool users who also share cross paths, don't
   hide location, and reported a location in the **last 30 minutes** inside a lat/lon bounding box
   (`crossings.ts:18`); then keeps those within **1 km** by haversine (`crossedIds`, `crossings.ts:31`).
3. Each pair is stored once, undirected (`user_a < user_b`, `crossingPair`, `crossings.ts:13`) in
   table `crossings` (`schema.ts:311`). The count goes up by 1 **only if the last crossing is older
   than 6 h** (`repository.ts:530-544`), so staying near someone all evening is one crossing.
   Only the count, time and city-level area label are stored, never the coordinates of the meeting.
4. At ranking time the count feeds the boost (section 9.1). On the detail screen, the count and place
   are shown only if both sides' privacy allows it (`shouldShowCrossPaths`, `service.ts:168`).

`crossingBlockers` (`crossings.ts:53`) lists why two people would *not* cross; the backoffice uses
it for debugging.

## 13. Synthetic agents and the simulation engine

About 2,000 synthetic profiles (seeded from `synthetic_users.csv` and `synthetic_interactions.csv`,
derived from the speed-dating dataset, `apps/api/scripts/seed.ts:179`) act as **agents** so the app
has a living population and so the recommender can be evaluated in simulation.

### 13.1 The tick (`runTick`, `apps/api/src/modules/simulation/engine.ts:41`)

1. **Replies** (`engine.ts:67-82`): agents answer up to 200 pending likes they received (from real
   users or other agents), but only likes that came from an acted-on recommendation, so the one-sided
   historical dataset rows are left alone (`apps/api/src/modules/simulation/repository.ts:13`).
   If a reply makes a match with a real user, that user gets a push.
2. **Acting** (`engine.ts:84-108`): 50 random agents (`AGENT.defaultTickLimit`) get a batch if they
   have none pending, generated with `syntheticOnly: true` (agents only see agents, so an agent never
   removes itself from a real user's pool by deciding first), and decide on every candidate.
3. **Real users** (`engine.ts:110-116`): `ensureBatch` for every real, discoverable user, with a
   push when a new drop is ready.

**When it runs**: Worker cron `0 8,20 * * *` (08:00 and 20:00 UTC) and `*/10 * * * *` for walkers
(`apps/api/wrangler.jsonc:16`), both only if `SIMULATION_ENABLED = "true"`
(`apps/api/src/index.ts:18`; currently `"true"` in `wrangler.jsonc`). Staff can run one now with
`POST /staff/simulation/tick` (`apps/api/src/modules/staff/service.ts:169`).

### 13.2 How an agent decides (working copy)

`decide` (`engine.ts:53-65`):

```
hidden  = hiddenProfile(agent)                                   agent-decision.ts:63
u       = agentUtility(hidden.profile → other, hidden.weights)   agent-utility.ts:69
P(accept) = sigmoid( (u − 0.71) / 0.02 )                         agent-decision.ts:31
accept  = seededRandom(tickId:agentId:otherId) < P(accept)       agent-decision.ts:80
```

- **Hidden profile** (`hiddenProfile`, `apps/api/src/lib/simulation/agent-decision.ts:63`): the agent's stored
  interests, `pref` split and component weights, each blended with a random draw seeded by the agent
  id: `(1 − hiddenNoise) · stored + hiddenNoise · random`, `hiddenNoise = 0.5`
  (`agent-decision.ts:13`). Stable across ticks and never stored, so the recommender **cannot see**
  what the agent really wants, but its stored profile is a noisy signal of it. That is the realistic
  situation the recommender is in with real users.
- **Frozen utility** (`apps/api/src/lib/simulation/agent-utility.ts`): a copy of the scoring formula **as of
  commit `a7ad2c3`**: plain (uncentered) cosine for interests and preference, a renormalized weighted
  mean of all six terms, its own base weights `AGENT_BASE_WEIGHTS` (`agent-utility.ts:18`). The point
  (header comment, `agent-utility.ts:10-15`): changing the recommender must not change what agents
  want, otherwise simulation runs are not comparable and the evaluation becomes circular. **Never tune
  this together with the recommender.**
- **Acceptance curve** (`agent-decision.ts:14-24`): utility over random synthetic pairs has median
  0.67, sd 0.05. Threshold 0.71 and temperature 0.02 give ~26% overall acceptance; the top utility
  quartile accepts ~71% and the bottom ~1%. The old 0.55 / 0.08 accepted ~80% of everything, so
  decisions carried almost no taste.
- **Reproducible**: every random draw is seeded (`seededRandom`, FNV-1a + mulberry32,
  `agent-decision.ts:40`), so the same `tickId` gives the same decisions.

### 13.3 How agents decided in the committed version (`a7ad2c3`)

In the committed code the engine called `agentAccepts(unilateral(hidden.profile, other, hidden.weights), …)`,
i.e. agents decided with the **recommender's own one-way score** on their hidden profile, with
threshold 0.55 and temperature 0.08. That makes evaluation partly circular: the recommender is
graded by a judge built from its own formula. The staged change replaces it with the frozen
`agentUtility`. If you report simulation results, say which version produced them.

### 13.4 What is still shared between agent and recommender

Even in the working copy, the hidden profile only perturbs **interests, pref and weights**. The bio
embedding, location, self-ratings, mood and daily answer the agent "feels" are the same ones the
recommender sees, and the bio, geo, mood and daily-answer functions are literally the same code.
The agent's taste is therefore still correlated with what the recommender can compute. That is
intended (a recommender must have *some* signal), but say it in the thesis: simulated hit rates are
an optimistic upper bound for real users.

### 13.5 Location walkers (`apps/api/src/modules/simulation/walkers.ts`)

A staff tool to test crossings without a second phone: synthetic profiles are assigned to walk
around a target user (`assignWalkers`, `walkers.ts:43`), take a random step every 10 minutes
(`stepWalkers`, `walkers.ts:115`), and each step records crossings like a real device would
(`placeWalker`, `walkers.ts:27`). Only synthetic profiles can be moved. Removing a walker restores
its original location; the crossings stay.

## 14. Configuration and ablation switches

The effective config is the **saved `matching_config` row (id 1) if one exists, otherwise the code
defaults** (`resolveMatchingConfig`, `apps/api/src/lib/matching/matching-config.ts:34`). Staff edit it on the
backoffice **Matching rules** page (`GET`/`PUT /staff/matching-config`).

| Setting | Code default | DB column default | Ablation value |
|---|---|---|---|
| batch size | 4 | (required) | |
| cooldown hours | 24 (or `REC_COOLDOWN_HOURS`) | (required) | |
| weights bio / interest / geo / pref / mood / answer | 0.30 / 0.25 / 0.15 / 0.15 / 0.05 / 0.10 | mood and answer default 0 | set one to 0 to drop a term |
| cross-paths λ | 0.5 | (required) | 0 = no boost |
| fairness μ | 1 | **0** (`schema.ts:513`) | 0 = no fairness |
| exposure cap | null | null | e.g. 3 |
| exposure window days | 7 | 7 | |
| preference λ (learning) | 0.5 | **0** (`schema.ts:518`) | 0 = stated only |
| calibration | on | not configurable | `calibration = null` in an offline script |
| agent threshold / temperature / hiddenNoise | 0.71 / 0.02 / 0.5 | code only (`agent-decision.ts:8-29`) | report with results |

**Watch out**: a row saved before fairness and learning existed has `fairness_mu = 0` and
`preference_lambda = 0`, so in that database **fairness and learned preferences are off** even though
the code defaults are on. Always read the backoffice Matching rules page (or
`SELECT * FROM matching_config`) and record the values with every result.

`generateRecommendations` also takes every one of these as an option (`generator.ts:27-43`), which is
how an evaluation script runs ablations without touching the saved config.

## 15. A worked example

Viewer *u* and candidate *v*, default weights, calibrated. Neither has checked in a mood or answered
today (both z = 0).

| Term | u→v value | z | v→u value | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| interest | 0.75 | +1.22 | 0.75 | +1.22 |
| geo (≈5.6 km) | 0.80 | +1.36 | 0.80 | +1.36 |
| pref | 0.60 | +0.48 | 0.40 | −0.43 |
| bio | 0.55 | +0.82 | 0.55 | +0.82 |

- `S(u→v) = (0.25·1.22 + 0.15·1.36 + 0.15·0.48 + 0.30·0.82) / sqrt(0.21) = 0.826 / 0.458 = 1.80`,
  so `p = sigmoid(1.80) = 0.858`.
- `S(v→u) = 0.690 / 0.458 = 1.51`, so `q = 0.818`.
- Reciprocal = `2 · 0.858 · 0.818 / (0.858 + 0.818) = 0.838`.
- 3 crossings: boost `0.5 · 0.632 = 0.316`; `logit(0.838) = 1.64`, so boosted = `sigmoid(1.96) = 0.876`
  (this is the stored score).
- Shown 5 times this week, μ = 1: penalty `1 · 5/10 = 0.5`; fair = `sigmoid(1.96 − 0.5) = 0.811`
  (used only to rank, not stored).

You can reproduce numbers like these in a unit test by building two `ScoringProfile` objects and
calling `reciprocalScore`, `applyCrossPathsBoost` and `exposurePenalty`.

## 16. Testing the engine

### 16.1 Unit tests (fast, no database)


(Run on your Mac. The `node_modules` there are built for macOS and won't run in a Linux sandbox.)

Relevant test files: `src/lib/scoring.test.ts` (every term, calibration, reciprocity, boost),
`fairness.test.ts`, `preference-learning.test.ts`, `agent-decision.test.ts`,
`agent-utility.test.ts`, `crossings.test.ts`, `partner-filters.test.ts`, `bio-embedding.test.ts`
(mocked Workers AI), `match-reasons.test.ts`, `recommendation-cadence.test.ts`, and
`src/modules/recommendations/generator.test.ts` (ranking, liker slots, shuffle).

Good properties to assert (most are already covered; add the missing ones):

- **Neutrality**: a missing term gives z = 0; an empty profile gives interest = 0.5.
- **Symmetry**: `reciprocalScore(u, v).reciprocal === reciprocalScore(v, u).reciprocal`.
- **Harmonic-mean dominance**: lowering *q* lowers the reciprocal more than lowering an arithmetic mean would.
- **Context only lifts**: adding a "low" mood never lowers the score versus no check-in.
- **Monotonicity**: more crossings never lowers the boosted score; more exposures never raise `fair`.
- **Ablation identity**: λ = 0, μ = 0 with no cap, preference λ = 0 reproduce the base ranking exactly.
- **Determinism**: same `tickId` gives the same agent decisions; same `viewerId:shownAt` gives the same order.

### 16.3 Useful SQL

```sql
-- current effective config (if empty, code defaults apply)
SELECT * FROM matching_config;

-- one user's current batch, score order (rank is the shuffled display position)
SELECT candidate_id, score, rank, shown_at, acted_on
FROM recommendations WHERE user_id = :id ORDER BY score DESC;

-- embeddings coverage
SELECT count(*) FILTER (WHERE e.user_profile_id IS NOT NULL) AS with_vec, count(*) AS total
FROM user_profiles p LEFT JOIN user_embeddings e ON e.user_profile_id = p.id
WHERE p.bio IS NOT NULL AND trim(p.bio) <> '';

-- acceptance and match rate of recommended pairs, by viewer type
SELECT v.is_synthetic,
       count(*) AS shown,
       avg((i.dec_a = 1)::int) AS accept_rate,
       avg((i.match)::int)     AS match_rate
FROM recommendations r
JOIN user_profiles v ON v.id = r.user_id
LEFT JOIN interactions i ON i.user_a = r.user_id AND i.user_b = r.candidate_id
WHERE r.acted_on
GROUP BY v.is_synthetic;
```

## 17. Evaluating the engine

The planned script (`research/eval/run.ts`, task W3.2 in `docs/path-a-implementation.md`) **does not
exist yet**. This section is the recipe for it. Write it in TypeScript and import
`apps/api/src/lib/matching/scoring.ts`, `fairness.ts` and `preference-learning.ts` directly, so the thesis
reports the deployed engine, not a re-implementation.

### 17.1 What to evaluate

| Question | Metric | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Does the score rank real matches high? | reciprocal hit@k, precision@k, recall@k, (MRR, NDCG@k) | 17.3 |
| Is it better than chance? | same metrics for a random ranker | 17.3 |
| Does each part help? | metric deltas per ablation | 17.5 |
| Is exposure spread fairly? | Gini of exposures, coverage | 17.4 |
| Does it work in a live loop? | acceptance rate, match rate per shown pair, learned-vs-hidden taste | 17.6 |

### 17.2 Offline protocol (ranking held-out matches)

1. **Load** all discoverable profiles with `listScoringProfilesByIds` (gives the exact scoring
   shape, including synthetic mood and answer for the evaluation date), and all `interactions`.
2. **Positives**: for viewer *u*, `M_u` = people *u* matched with (`match = true`). Only evaluate
   users with at least one match.
3. **Candidate set** `C_u`: everyone in the same `looking_for` pool passing `mutualFilterMatch`
   (`partner-filters.ts:41`). **Do not** apply `noInteractionWith`: production removes people you
   already decided on, but offline those are exactly the positives you want to find.
4. **Temporal split for learning**: learned preferences must come only from decisions made **before**
   the held-out matches (for example split interactions by `created_at`, train on the earlier 80%,
   test on the later 20%). Learning from the same likes you are predicting is leakage.
5. **Rank** `C_u` by `reciprocalScore(u, v, weights, calibration).reciprocal`, then
   `applyCrossPathsBoost` with each pair's crossing count. Ignore the exposure reranker for the
   relevance metrics (it is evaluated separately), or run it as its own variant.
6. **Score** the top-k with the metrics below, for k = 4 (the real batch size) and 10, 20.

### 17.3 Ranking metrics

For each viewer *u*, with `T_u(k)` the top-k list:

- **Hit@k** = 1 if `T_u(k) ∩ M_u ≠ ∅`, else 0. Report the mean over users.
- **Precision@k** = `|T_u(k) ∩ M_u| / k`.
- **Recall@k** = `|T_u(k) ∩ M_u| / |M_u|`.
- **MRR** = mean of `1 / rank of the first match` (0 if none in the list).
- **NDCG@k** (optional) = `Σ_{i∈T_u(k)} rel_i / log2(i + 1)` divided by the ideal, with `rel = 1` for a match.

Also report **one-way variants** to show why reciprocity matters: rank by `p(u→v)` only and measure
how often *u*'s **likes** are found (one-way hit@k) versus how often **matches** are found. A good
reciprocal ranker should lose a little on likes and win on matches.

**Random baseline**: shuffle `C_u` with a fixed seed and compute the same metrics; average over
several seeds. Expected precision@k under random is `|M_u| / |C_u|`, which is a useful sanity check.

Report means with **95% bootstrap confidence intervals** over users, and use a paired test (paired
bootstrap or Wilcoxon signed-rank) when comparing two variants on the same users.

### 17.4 Fairness metrics

- **Gini of exposure** with `gini()` (`fairness.ts:56`): 0 = everyone shown equally, close to 1 =
  a few profiles get everything. Two sources:
  - **Online**: counts from `recommendation_exposures` over a window.
  - **Offline**: how often each profile appears in everyone's top-k in your simulated ranking.
- **Include zero-exposure profiles.** `gini()` only sees the numbers you pass. Build the vector over
  **all eligible profiles** (0 for never shown), or the inequality is understated.
- **Separate real and synthetic viewers.** Exposure rows are logged for agent batches too
  (`repository.ts:425`), so filter by the viewer's `is_synthetic` when you report the real-user
  number.
- **Coverage@k**: share of eligible profiles that appear in at least one top-k list.
- Compare **μ = 0, no cap** versus **μ = 1** (and a cap, e.g. 3), and report the trade-off with
  hit@k (fairness usually costs a little relevance).

```sql
-- exposure counts over the last 7 days, including never-shown profiles, real viewers only
SELECT p.id, count(x.id) AS exposures
FROM user_profiles p
LEFT JOIN recommendation_exposures x
  ON x.candidate_id = p.id
 AND x.shown_at >= now() - interval '7 days'
 AND x.viewer_id IN (SELECT id FROM user_profiles WHERE NOT is_synthetic)
WHERE p.discoverable
GROUP BY p.id;
```

### 17.5 Ablations to run

Each variant changes one thing from the full model:

| Variant | How |
|---|---|
| Full model | defaults |
| No calibration | `calibration = null` |
| One-way only | rank by `p(u→v)` |
| Arithmetic mean | `(p + q) / 2` instead of harmonic |
| Drop a term | its weight = 0 (do each of the six) |
| No cross-paths boost | λ = 0 |
| No learning | preference λ = 0 |
| No fairness | μ = 0, cap = null |
| Random | seeded shuffle |

Then a small **weight sweep** (for example a coarse grid or random search over the simplex) and
record the best weights. Keep the sweep honest: choose weights on one split and report on another.

### 17.6 Simulation-based evaluation

Real-user data will be small (a usability study), so the agents are the main way to see the engine
work in a closed loop.

1. Record the config, agent threshold, temperature and `hiddenNoise`, the code commit, and a start
   time.
2. Run ticks (cron or `POST /staff/simulation/tick` repeatedly). Use fixed `tickId`s for
   reproducibility if you call `runTick` from a script.
3. Measure, for interactions created after the start time:
   - **acceptance rate** of shown candidates (`accepts / decisions`, from the tick stats or SQL),
   - **match rate per shown pair**,
   - **hit@4 per batch** = share of agent batches with at least one match,
   - **exposure Gini** over the run,
   - **learning recovery**: cosine between each agent's learned direction (`learnedPreference`) and
     its hidden `pref` (`hiddenProfile(agent).profile.pref`, centered), as decisions accumulate.
     It should rise with *n*.
4. Compare variants by running the same number of ticks from the same starting state. Since the
   population changes during a run (pools shrink as pairs are decided), reset or reseed between
   variants.
5. **Do not** report agents as participants; they are synthetic (thesis note in
   `docs/path-a-implementation.md`).
6. Mention the shared-signal caveat (section 13.4).

The ideal simulated upper bound is also computable: for each agent, rank its pool by its **own**
`agentUtility` on the hidden profile. The gap between that oracle and the recommender is a clean
measure of how much taste the recommender recovers.

## 18. Known caveats and things to check before reporting numbers

1. **Staged, uncommitted changes.** The working copy differs a lot from `a7ad2c3` (top of this
   document). Commit and deploy before collecting live data, and note the commit in the thesis.
2. **Effective config.** If a `matching_config` row exists, its values win; its DB defaults leave
   fairness and learning **off** (section 14).
3. **Calibration population.** `COMPONENT_CALIBRATION` was measured on mostly synthetic pairs. Re-run
   `db:component-stats` after real users join or the population changes.
4. **Embedding model.** Run the bio backfill so every vector is bge-small. Mixed models make the bio
   term meaningless.
5. **Synthetic daily answers** come from a fixed list, so two agents often have the **identical**
   answer (z clipped to 3) and the daily-answer term behaves very differently for agents than for
   people. Report it, or evaluate with and without the term.
6. **Daily answer is lexical**, not semantic (`scoring.ts:290`).
7. **Learned preferences use all decisions**, including the seeded historical dataset rows and the
   agents' simulated ones (`repository.ts:551`). Fine for the product, but for offline evaluation
   control it with the temporal split (section 17.2).
8. **`rank` is the shuffled display order**, and `score` excludes the fairness penalty. To rebuild the
   engine's ordering from the table, sort by `score`, and remember reserved likers were placed first.
9. **No hard distance filter** today (`maxKm` is never passed); distance only acts through geo.
10. **Exposure rows include agent batches**; filter by viewer type.
11. **Small leftover**: at `repository.ts:549` an orphan doc comment ("Profile ids whose like on the
    viewer is still unanswered.") sits above `listPreferenceDecisions`; it belongs to
    `listPendingLikerIds` at `:586`. Harmless, but worth tidying.
